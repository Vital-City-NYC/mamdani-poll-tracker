## Methodological comparability

Three different questions are in play. Do not plot them as one series.

1. Job approval, binary.
   - Marist: "Do you approve or disapprove of the job Zohran Mamdani is doing as mayor?" plus a strength follow-up. "Unsure" is volunteered only, which keeps it low among RV (20%), though it reached 23% among adults.
   - Emerson: "Do you approve or disapprove of the job Zohran Mamdani is doing as Mayor?" with an offered "Neutral or no opinion" option. Offering a neutral choice pulls both approve and disapprove down: 30% chose neutral. Some of the gap between Emerson's 43 and Marist's 48/49 likely comes from this design rather than from a change in opinion. The two polls were fielded a week apart.
   - Honan: "Do you approve or disapprove of the job Zohran Mamdani is doing as Mayor of New York City?" It publishes only the Jewish/non-Jewish split.
2. Performance rating, four-point scale.
   - Suffolk: "How would you rate the job performance of Zohran Mamdani as mayor?" Excellent / good / fair / poor, with order rotated. Suffolk counts fair with poor as the negative side. "Fair" is not disapproval: many respondents who would say "approve" on a binary question choose "fair." Suffolk's 34% fair-or-poor is therefore not comparable to a disapproval figure, and the 57% excellent-or-good is a different quantity from approval.
3. Favorability, about the person rather than the job.
   - Marist: "In general, do you have a very favorable, somewhat favorable, somewhat unfavorable or very unfavorable impression of Zohran Mamdani?"
   - Siena: "Do you have a favorable or unfavorable opinion about Zohran Mamdani?"
   - Quinnipiac's figure also includes "haven't heard enough." Favorability usually runs higher than job approval for the same person, as in Marist: 55 favorable against 48 approve, from the same respondents.

Population differences: Marist reports adults and RV, Emerson and Suffolk RV, Honan LV. Siena and Quinnipiac are statewide RV or LV with an NYC regional cut. Siena switched from RV to LV in August, which affects the statewide trend.

Mode differences: Suffolk is live phone only. Marist mixes phone, text and online. Emerson mixes email, text-to-web and online panel. Honan is text-to-web only.
