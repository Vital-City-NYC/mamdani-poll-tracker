# Bloomberg, first 12 months: job-approval polls

Mayor Michael Bloomberg was inaugurated January 1, 2002. This file covers citywide polls fielded January through December 2002, verified on the pollster's own page. Numbers below are copied from those pages; nothing comes from news coverage, Wikipedia or aggregators. Compiled October 2, 2026. Machine-readable version: bloomberg.json.

## Verified polls

### Quinnipiac University, fielded January 29 to February 4, 2002 (released February 6)
Registered voters, n=1,008, margin of error 3.1 points. Approve 65, disapprove 6, don't know 29.
URL (fetched direct): https://poll.qu.edu/poll-release?releaseid=455
Quote: "1. Do you approve or disapprove of the way Michael Bloomberg is handling his job as Mayor? ... Approve 65% ... Disapprove 6 ... DK/NA 29." And: "From January 29 - February 4, Quinnipiac University surveyed 1,008 New York City registered voters, with a margin of error of +/- 3.1 percent."
Note: the release headline is about President Bush's approval in the city; Bloomberg's approval is question 1 in the tables. The March 27 release restates it: "In a February 6 poll by the independent Quinnipiac University, Bloomberg got a 65 - 6 percent approval, with 29 percent undecided."

### Quinnipiac University, fielded March 19 to 25, 2002 (released March 27)
Registered voters, n=1,038, margin of error 3 points. Approve 62, disapprove 16, don't know 22.
URL (fetched direct): https://poll.qu.edu/poll-release?releaseid=457
Quote: "By a 62 - 16 percent margin, with 22 percent undecided, New York City voters approve of the job Michael Bloomberg is doing as Mayor, according to a Quinnipiac University poll released today." And: "From March 19 - 25, Quinnipiac University surveyed 1,038 New York City registered voters, with a margin of error of +/- 3 percent."

### Quinnipiac University, fielded July 8 to 15, 2002 (released July 18)
Registered voters, n=932, margin of error 3.3 points. Approve 57, disapprove 23, don't know 19 (the published table sums to 99).
URL (fetched direct): https://poll.qu.edu/poll-release?releaseid=461
Quote: "Voters approve 57 - 23 percent of the job Bloomberg is doing, compared to 62 - 16 percent in a March 27 poll by the independent Quinnipiac University." And: "From July 8 - 15, Quinnipiac University surveyed 932 New York City registered voters, with a margin of error of +/- 3.3 percent."

### Quinnipiac University, fielded November 13 to 19, 2002 (released November 21)
Registered voters, n=1,004, margin of error 3.1 points. Approve 41, disapprove 46, don't know 13.
URL (fetched direct): https://poll.qu.edu/poll-release?releaseid=462
Quote: "New York City's budget woes have whacked Mayor Bloomberg's approval rating, driving it to a negative 41 - 46 percent - the lowest score for a New York City Mayor since Rudolph Giuliani's 37 - 57 percent rating April 19, 2000, according to a Quinnipiac University poll released today." And: "From November 13 - 19, Quinnipiac University surveyed 1,004 New York City registered voters, with a margin of error of +/- 3.1 percent."
Note: two sibling releases from the same field, November 25 (releaseid=464) and December 4 (releaseid=465), say Quinnipiac "surveyed 1,322 New York City" voters. The approval release's 1,004 is recorded.

### Marist College Institute for Public Opinion, fielded February 26 to March 4, 2002 (released March 12)
Type: performance (excellent/good/fair/poor), not approve/disapprove. Registered voters, n=898, margin of error 3.5 points (1,280 residents interviewed overall). Excellent plus good 50 (excellent 8, good 42), fair 29, poor 6, unsure 15.
URL (fetched via web.archive.org; Marist's own 2002 release page, capture of June 9, 2002): https://web.archive.org/web/20020609151522/http://www.maristpoll.marist.edu/docs/nycpolls/020312my.htm
Quote: "Mayor Michael Bloomberg's approval rating is 50% among New York City's registered voters. 8% of the electorate rates the job he is doing as excellent, 42% as good, 29% as fair, and 6% as poor. 15% are unsure." And: "This survey was conducted from February 26th through March 4th, 2002. 1,280 residents of New York City were interviewed in proportion to the population in each borough. ... 898 registered voters in New York City were interviewed in proportion to the enrollment in each borough. The results for registered voters are statistically significant at +/- 3.5%."
Corroboration on Marist's live site: the "Bloomberg Approval Rating Over Time" table published with later releases lists "March 2002 50% 8% 42% 29% 6% 15%" for NYC registered voters, for example https://maristpoll.marist.edu/wp-content/misc/nycpolls/c110104/Bloomberg/Bloomberg%20Approval%20Rating_Over%20Time.pdf (fetched direct). No sponsor is named on the 2002 release.

### CBS News/New York Times, fielded June 4 to 9, 2002 (released June 10)
Adults, n=940, margin of error 3 points. Approve 48, disapprove 26, no opinion 26 (derived; CBS prints only approve and disapprove).
URL (fetched direct; CBS's own page, "New York City: Nine Months Later"): https://www.cbsnews.com/news/new-york-city-nine-months-later/
Quote: "Six months into his term, 48% approve of the job he is doing as Mayor, and 26% disapprove. This is similar to the evaluations New Yorkers gave former Mayor Rudolph Giuliani six months after he took office." And: "This poll was conducted among a citywide random sample of 940 adults, interviewed by telephone June 4-9, 2002. The error due to sampling could be plus or minus three percentage points for results based on the entire sample."
Note: the 2002 URL (cbsnews.com/stories/2002/06/10/opinion/polls/main511729.shtml) redirects to the live page; the Wayback capture of August 19, 2002 matches. ICPSR's catalog entry for this study (3697) gives field dates of June 4 to 8; CBS's page says June 4 to 9 and is used.

### CBS News/New York Times, fielded August 25 to 29, 2002 (released September 10)
Adults, n=1,008, margin of error 3 points. Approve 47, disapprove 31, no opinion 22 (derived; CBS says "more than one in five").
URL (fetched direct; CBS's own page, "New York City: One Year Later"): https://www.cbsnews.com/news/new-york-city-one-year-later/
Also on Wayback, capture of September 11, 2002: https://web.archive.org/web/20020911173209/http://www.cbsnews.com/stories/2002/09/10/opinion/polls/main521387.shtml
Quote: "47% of New Yorkers approve of Mayor Michael Bloomberg's handling of his job, unchanged since June. 31% now disapprove, and more than one in five still offer no opinion." And: "This poll was conducted among a citywide random sample of 1008 adults, interviewed by telephone August 25-29, 2002. The error due to sampling could be plus or minus three percentage points for results based on the entire sample."

## Unverified or ruled out

- Marist/NY1 2002 beyond March: no other 2002 Marist poll of Bloomberg exists on Marist's pages. Marist's own trend table goes from March 2002 straight to April 2004, and Wayback captures of Marist's 2002 site list only the March 12 Bloomberg release and a March 8 World Trade Center release for the city.
- Quinnipiac June 2002 (~57%): there is no June 2002 NYC release. The 57-23 reading is July 18 (field July 8 to 15).
- Quinnipiac August/September 2002: none. Every poll.qu.edu release ID from 1 to 1000 was scanned by title (seven IDs return an empty redirect). The 2002 NYC releases are February 6-7, March 27-28, April 1, July 17-18, November 21-25 and December 4. The next NYC approval reading after November 21, 2002 is February 26, 2003 (releaseid=352).
- Quinnipiac December 2002: the December 4 release reuses the November 13 to 19 field and does not re-ask approval.
- The Times' own article pages for the June and August 2002 NYT/CBS polls were not found; CBS's pages were used instead. ICPSR pages (studies 3697 and 3703) return 403 to this tool.
- NYT New York State poll, October 2002 (ICPSR 3708): statewide, out of scope.

## Method
Quinnipiac: poll.qu.edu/poll-release?releaseid=N serves releases back to the 1990s; IDs are not chronological (2002 runs roughly 410 to 478, 2003 roughly 352 to 366). Marist: the 2002 site is only on web.archive.org, located through Wayback CDX queries for maristpoll.marist.edu; the March 2002 figures are cross-checked against Marist's own later trend PDFs. CBS: 2002 poll pages located through Wayback CDX queries for cbsnews.com/stories/2002/*/opinion/polls/ and then confirmed live on cbsnews.com. The two CBS unsure figures are derived by subtraction. The Marist reading is a grade, not approval, and should not be charted on the same axis as approve/disapprove without saying so.
